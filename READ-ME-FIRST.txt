SPACECADET - meeting attention assistant
========================================

WHAT IT IS
  You lose focus in a meeting. You glance at this. Two seconds later you know
  what is being discussed, and whether someone just asked you something - what
  they meant by it, and what kind of answer they are waiting for.

HOW TO OPEN IT
  1. Save spacecadet.html somewhere you will keep it (Desktop or Documents).
  2. Double-click it. It opens in Chrome or Edge.
  3. Setup opens by itself and asks two things:
       - What people call you. The name alert cannot fire without this, which
         is why it is the first thing asked. Add nicknames too.
       - Where transcription happens. Read that screen; the short version is
         under WHERE YOUR AUDIO GOES below.
  4. Press L, tick the box confirming you have told the room, and click Allow
     on the microphone prompt.

  There is nothing to install. No account, no API key, no sign-up.

KEYS
  L    start / stop listening
  C    catch me up (copies a prompt, opens claude.ai)
  B    bookmark this moment
  T    read the full transcript
  R    meeting snapshot
  E    export the meeting as a Markdown file
  D    dismiss the alert
  V    discreet mode (compact, no colour, quiet alert - off by default)
  S    settings
  1-9  mark who is speaking, in the order they appear in the bar at the top
  Esc  blank the screen instantly. Press it again to bring it back.

  Esc works from anywhere, always, including while you are typing. Whether it
  also stops the microphone is a setting, off by default.

  The number keys are the ones worth learning. Tapping a name as each person
  starts talking is what gives a to-do a real owner in the export.

IF THIS IS A LEVEL 10 (and only then)
  The EOS agenda segments are OFF unless you ask for them. Most meetings are
  not an L10 and the row of jargon is just clutter.

  To turn them on: Settings -> This meeting -> Type -> "Leadership / Level 10".
  You then get the segment row - Check-in, Scorecard, Rocks, Headlines, To-dos,
  IDS, Conclude. Tap the one you are in and everything captured from then on is
  stamped with it, so the export reads as an agenda log rather than a word
  cloud. IDS runs a clock on the issue being discussed.

  That setting also adds "Copy for Strety" to the export menu, which gives you
  a block that pastes straight into the matching To-dos, Issues and Headlines
  lists. Switch the type back to Standard and all of it disappears again.

  To-dos you never tick carry forward into your next meeting automatically,
  whichever type of meeting it is.

WHERE YOUR AUDIO GOES
  There is no server. This is one static file; nothing is uploaded, logged, or
  stored anywhere except your own browser.

  On first run it asks you to choose:

    On this device      Audio never leaves your laptop. Needs a one-time
                        ~40 MB download, which some office networks block.
                        Text quality is lower - good enough to follow along.

    Google speech       Your meeting audio is sent to Google to be
                        transcribed, the same service phone dictation uses.
                        Nothing to install, noticeably more accurate.

  If you want nothing at all to leave the machine: choose on-device, and set
  the summarizer switch to "local". The app then blocks its own outbound calls
  and logs every blocked attempt so you can verify it.

  For a personnel matter, an investigation, a board executive session, or
  anything involving a minor: turn on "Sensitive meeting" in Settings. That
  hard-disables export, the claude.ai handoff and every API call at once. Or
  better, do not run it at all in those meetings.

  It asks you to confirm you have told the room before it records, every
  session, and shows a recording indicator that discreet mode cannot hide.

  Stored meetings are purged automatically after a number of days you set.

  The whole thing is one readable file. Open it in Notepad, or read it on
  GitHub, before you trust it with a microphone.

WHO SAID WHAT
  Click a name in the bar at the top, or press its number, as each person
  speaks. What follows is attributed to them until you change it.

  You can also record about eight seconds of each person's voice once, and it
  will try to recognise them in later meetings. What it stores is a short list
  of numbers describing pitch and tone - never recorded audio - and none of it
  is ever sent anywhere. Guesses are marked with a "?" and never overwrite a
  name you picked yourself. It handles a handful of clearly different voices
  in decent audio and gets confused by similar voices and speakerphones.

TWO THINGS THAT MIGHT GO WRONG
  1. Chrome asks for the microphone every single time you open it. That is
     unavoidable for a file opened from disk - a local file has no web address
     for Chrome to remember a permission against. Opening the hosted version
     over https fixes it permanently.

  2. "Speech service unreachable" means your network blocks Google's speech
     service, which many corporate proxies do. Switch the engine to on-device
     in Settings. That needs a one-off ~40 MB model download, which some
     networks also block. If both are blocked, transcription cannot run on
     that network at all.

REMOTE MEETING AUDIO (Windows only)
  To hear people on the far end of a Teams or Zoom call rather than just your
  own room, set the audio source to "System / tab audio", share the ENTIRE
  SCREEN, and tick "Also share system audio" in the picker. A window share
  carries no audio track. Chrome on macOS cannot do this at all.

  Note that a shared system-audio stream is one mixed channel, so automatic
  voice recognition does not work with it. Microphone only.

WHAT AN API KEY WOULD COST
  You do not need one. The default mode uses your own claude.ai subscription
  and this page never calls an API.

  If you do add a key, at the default 90-second interval it runs about 43
  cents per hour of meeting on Sonnet - roughly $220 a year at ten hours a
  week. Haiku is about a third of that, Opus about $370. Those are upper
  bounds: nothing is sent when nobody is talking, and doubling the interval
  halves the bill. Settings shows the live number for your own settings, and
  there is a per-meeting spend cap that stops it dead.

REQUIREMENTS
  Chrome or Edge. Built and tuned for Windows.
