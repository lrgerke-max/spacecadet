What it does

  • Listens to your microphone or the meeting's system audio and transcribes it in your browser.
  • Shows the current topic in text you can read at a glance, with the last stretch of speech under it.
  • Raises an alert the moment your name or a question aimed at you shows up — with the exchange it came out of, so you can answer cold. That runs on plain pattern matching, so it is instant and works with no account and no network.
  • Tracks to-dos, issues, decisions and unfamiliar terms as they are said, each with an owner where one can be worked out. Unticked to-dos carry into the next meeting.
  • Catch me up builds a prompt, copies it, and opens claude.ai — your own subscription pays, and this page never calls an API.

Who said what

  • Click a name, or press its number key, as each person speaks. What follows is attributed to them — that is what gives a to-do a real owner.
  • Optionally record about eight seconds of each voice once, and it will recognise them in later meetings. It stores a short list of numbers describing pitch and tone, never recorded audio, and never sends any of it anywhere. Guesses are marked with a ? and never overwrite a name you picked.

Where your audio goes

  • There is no server. This page is a single static file; nothing is uploaded, logged, or stored anywhere but your own browser.
  • On first run it asks where transcription should happen. Choose the browser's speech engine and your audio is sent to Google to be transcribed — that is Chrome's engine, not this app's choice. Choose on-device and nothing leaves the laptop.
  • Set the summarizer to local as well and the app hard-disables its own outbound calls, logging every blocked attempt so you can check.
  • A Sensitive meeting switch turns off export, the claude.ai handoff and every API call at once — for personnel matters, investigations or board executive session.
  • It asks you to confirm you have told the room before it records, every session, and shows a recording indicator that cannot be hidden.
  • The whole thing is one readable file. Open it in a text editor, or read it on GitHub, before you trust it with a microphone.

First run

  • Press S and enter your name — including whatever mangled spellings speech-to-text produces for it. The name alert cannot fire without this.
  • Add who is in the room in the same panel, then press 1–9 as each one speaks.
  • Press L and click Allow on the microphone prompt.
  • C catch me up · B bookmark · T transcript · R snapshot · E export · V discreet · Esc blank the screen instantly, again to bring it back.
  • For remote Teams or Zoom audio on Windows: switch the source to system audio, share the entire screen, and tick "Also share system audio". A window share carries no audio.
Open this page rather than a downloaded copy if you can. Served over https your browser remembers the microphone permission, so it stops asking at the start of every meeting. Opened from a file on disk it still works, but Chrome re-prompts every single time — that is a browser rule, not a bug.
