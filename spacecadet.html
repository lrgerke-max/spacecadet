      Catch me up

      Set up SpaceCadet

      Step 1 of 2

      SpaceCadet listens to the meeting and tells you what is being discussed — and the moment somebody asks you something. For that second part it has to know what you are called.

      Add every version people actually say, separated by commas — nicknames included. Speech-to-text mangles names, so if yours comes back wrong you can add the mangled spelling later in Settings.
      Puts them in the bar at the top so you can tag who is speaking with the number keys. You can add people any time.

      Speech has to be turned into text somewhere. This is the one decision that determines whether any audio leaves this laptop, so it is asked plainly. You can change it later in Settings.

      On this device Recommended
      Audio never leaves your laptop. Needs a one-time ~40 MB download, which some office networks block. Text quality is lower — good enough to follow along.
      Google speech service
      Your meeting audio is sent to Google to be transcribed, the same service phone dictation uses. Nothing to install and noticeably more accurate. Not appropriate for personnel, board, or other confidential conversations.
      Takes about fifteen seconds. Remembered on this device.

      Confirm before recording

      SpaceCadet is about to transcribe everyone audible in this meeting, not just you. Recording laws in several states require that every participant is told.

      • Say out loud that you are using a note-taking assistant.
      • Do not run this in a personnel matter, investigation, board executive session, or any conversation involving a minor.
      • If this is a confidential meeting, close this and switch on Sensitive meeting in Settings.
      Asked once per session, every session.

      Learn a voice

      Have them talk normally — reading anything is fine. About eight seconds is enough. Everyone else should stay quiet.

      Waiting for speech…
      Voice matching is approximate. It works with a handful of clearly different voices in decent audio, and gets confused by similar voices, crosstalk and speakerphones. Anything it guesses is marked with a ? — click the right name to correct it.

      Capturing the other people on the call

      Your microphone only hears the room. To transcribe people on the far end of a Teams or Zoom call, the browser has to share the computer's own sound.

      1. Press Start listening.
      2. In the picker, choose the Entire Screen tab — not Window, not Chrome Tab. A window share carries no audio at all.
      3. Tick Also share system audio at the bottom left. This is the step everyone misses.
      4. Click Share.
      Windows only. Chrome on macOS cannot capture system audio at all — there it is the microphone, or a single Chrome tab's audio, and nothing else.

      Settings

      Summarizer backend

      Anthropic API

      Read this. A file:// page offers no real secret protection — anything running in this browser profile can read localStorage. Use a personal, spend-limited key. Never a shared or production one. The key is stored on this machine only and is never sent anywhere except api.anthropic.com.
      A hard ceiling. Set to 0 to remove it.
      A call is skipped entirely when fewer than ~15 new words arrived, so silence costs nothing.

      Capture

      ~40 MB from cdn.jsdelivr.net + huggingface.co, cached by the browser afterwards. Required once before the Whisper engine can run. Corporate proxies often block it — if so the app falls back to Web Speech and says so.
      Whisper transcribes in blocks, so text arrives in bursts this far apart. Shorter = more responsive, more CPU.

      Who is in the room

      Click a name in the bar at the top (or press its number key) as each person speaks. What follows is attributed to them, which is what gives to-dos a real owner.
      Speaker profiles live only in this browser. Nothing about a voice is ever sent anywhere — the stored representation is a short list of numbers describing pitch and tone, not recorded audio.
      Needs a short voice sample per person, recorded while listening is running. Guessed names are shown with a ? and never overwrite a name you picked yourself. Microphone source only — a shared system-audio stream is one mixed channel, so it cannot be separated this way.

      This meeting

      Reads a saved .ics invite on this machine to fill in the title and attendees. Nothing is uploaded and no calendar account is connected — that would need a server this app deliberately does not have.
      Adds them to the roster so you can attribute speech, and helps resolve references like “ask Brooke about that”.

      Meeting sensitivity

      Turn this on for personnel matters, investigations, board executive session, or anything involving a minor. It hard-disables export, the claude.ai handoff, and every API call for as long as it is on.

      Attention

      Matched case-insensitively on whole words. Add the manglings speech-to-text actually produces for your name — that is where the misses come from.
      Esc always blanks the screen instantly either way. Leave this off to keep the transcript running while the screen is hidden. Turn it on if you would rather not have audio captured behind a blank screen.

      Analysis window

      Everything analytical — keywords, topic detection, the ambient summary — looks only at this much recent speech. The full meeting is still kept for export and catch-up.
      Cosine similarity between consecutive 60s term vectors. Higher = fires more often.

      Keyboard

      L start/stop listening   C catch me up   B bookmark this moment   T full transcript   R meeting snapshot   E export   D dismiss alert   V discreet   S settings   Esc panic-hide / restore
      1–9 mark who is speaking, in the order they appear in the bar at the top. This is the one you want under your fingers while the meeting runs — it is faster than reaching for the mouse, and it is what gives to-dos an owner.
      Keys are ignored while you are typing in a field, so a name with a c in it will not fire catch-up. Esc is the exception: it works from anywhere, always.

      Where your data goes

      This reflects your current settings, not a general claim. Change the summarizer or engine above and it updates.

      Retention

      Enforced on load: anything older is destroyed automatically rather than waiting for someone to remember.

      Configuration

      Downloads a JSON file so every branch can run the same configuration. The API key is never included.

      Data

      Settings and the in-flight meeting live in this browser’s localStorage, so an accidental refresh mid-meeting is not fatal, and it auto-purges on the schedule below.
      Where things actually go: with the Web Speech engine your audio is sent to Google to be transcribed. With the handoff summarizer, transcript text you copy goes to claude.ai under your own account. Choose on-device transcription and the local summarizer and nothing leaves this machine — the Network log records every attempt either way.
